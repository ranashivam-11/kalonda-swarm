# The array as a multi-agent system

Twelve rotors share one wind. Every node controls only its own yaw and reads
only its own sensors. A node gains by steering its wake *away from the rotor
behind it*, but that steering costs *it* power. This document explains why
local objectives fail, what the firmware optimises instead, and how a node
decides that a change is real when one four-minute trial is mostly noise.

Code: `firmware/src/ks_negotiate.c` (protocol and decisions),
`firmware/src/ks_node.c` (steering side, stability class),
`python/kalonda/swarm/game.py` (the game-theoretic model). Numbers quoted
here come from `data/simulated/` and are regenerated by the scripts named in
each section.

## 1. Players, actions, payoffs

* **Players**: units that have a downstream neighbour (for the reference
  3 × 4 grid in a westerly, the 8 units of the first two rows). Last-row
  units have nobody to steer for and never sacrifice.
* **Action**: a steering *magnitude* m_i ∈ [0°, 25°] per LUT bin. The sign
  is not negotiated. It follows geometry: the wake is deflected away from the
  side on which the nearest downstream rotor currently sits (`update_side`,
  with 0.1 D hysteresis). Negotiating a magnitude rather than a signed angle
  was the single largest improvement during development. Under the ±4°
  direction wander of a calm night, a fixed signed offset spends half its
  time pushing the wake *onto* the neighbour, and its expected gain was
  ≈ 0.
* **Payoff**: electrical power, averaged over the wind the bin actually
  sees. The game model takes the expectation over a Gaussian direction
  distribution (`tracked_power`). The firmware measures it.

A bin is a 10° wind sector × a stability class: the turbulence intensity of
the free-stream reference split at 0.10 with ±0.01 hysteresis. That gives 72
bins. Steering pays in stable air (narrow, persistent wakes) and hardly at
all in convective air.

## 2. Why purely local objectives settle on greedy

With the selfish utility u_i = P_i(m), yawing only ever costs the yawing
unit. Its own inflow is unchanged, while its own power falls as cos^p(γ).
For p = 2, that is 1.1 % at 6°, 4.3 % at 12° and 17.9 % at 25°. Best
responses therefore return m_i = 0 for every i: greedy operation is the
unique Nash equilibrium of the selfish game. Started at the cooperative
optimum, the same dynamics *unravel* it. Each unit's best response to
everyone else's sacrifice is to stop sacrificing.
(`tests/test_swarm.py::test_selfish_equilibrium_is_greedy_and_cooperation_unravels`
checks both on a 2 × 2 array.)

The price of anarchy, Φ(m\*)/Φ(m_NE), measures what that costs, with Φ the
total array power. For the reference grid on a calm night (4 m/s, TI 0.06,
±4° wander), `scripts/model_benchmarks.py` finds:

* **Price of anarchy 1.14.** The selfish equilibrium (greedy, efficiency
  0.545) gives up 12 % of what the cooperative optimum produces (25° on
  all eight steering units, efficiency 0.620).
* **Unravelling.** Best responses started at that optimum unravel to
  greedy in one round.
* **β sweep.** With the firmware's local utility, the best-response
  equilibrium moves from 0° at β = 0, to 5° at 0.25, to 12.5° at 0.5, to
  17.5–20° at 0.75, and to the optimum at β = 1 (`results_beta` figure).

## 3. Writing the sacrifice into the objective

The firmware's cooperative score for a trial that moves unit i from base
magnitude b to trial magnitude t is

    score_i = ΔP_i + β · Σ_{j ∈ D_i} ΔP_j

* ΔP_i (usually negative) is the unit's **measured sacrifice**.
* ΔP_j is the change reported by each downstream participant j. The
  participants are the units inside a widened top-hat wake cone, from
  `ks_geom.c`. The cone only chooses *whose power counts*. Nothing on the
  node predicts the benefit.

With β = 1, this is the marginal-contribution ("wonderful life") utility of
Wolpert & Tumer (2001), in the sense used by Marden, Arslan & Shamma (2009).
Such utilities make the game an exact potential game whose potential is the
total power, so every accepted unilateral improvement raises the array's
output. In the firmware the downstream set is a geometric cone rather than
"every unit the wake reaches". So the game is only *approximately* a
potential game, and `model_benchmarks.py` reports the defect in watts. β < 1
interpolates towards selfishness (experiment E2).

### The ledger

Every accepted step books the measured own change and the measured
downstream change: on the initiator (`led_self_w`, `led_down_w`) and on
each participant (`recv_w`). `python -m kalonda ledger` and
`kalonda.telemetry.ledger.ledger_from_trials` rebuild the same ledger
offline from the logs. A village can then see the transfer: who gave up
energy and who received it, with a Gini coefficient of the gains. Whether
front-row households should carry that cost is a social question the code
cannot answer. The code can make the transfer visible.

**The per-step ledger is biased, and the README figure does not use it.**
A step is *accepted* because its measured score came out high, so the
measurements booked on accepted steps over-state what was gained: the
winner's curse. In experiment E1, the booked own changes of three of the
four front-row units even come out positive (+82 W summed over accepted
steps), while their true sacrifice is negative. The
unbiased per-unit answer comes from comparing like with like, on the same
wind:

* in the field, the hours with steering toggled off against the hours with
  it on (`docs/field_protocol.md`);
* in the SIL, the run against greedy held fixed.

There, over the last two nights of E1:

| | front row | middle row | back row | array |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| power vs greedy | −9.0 W | +14.2 W | +6.7 W | **+11.9 W (+7.6 %)** |

The middle row both sacrifices and receives. Use the per-step ledger as
an *audit trail* (which steps were taken, on what evidence), not as the
account of who is owed what.

## 4. One trial: A–B–B–A on normalised power

A round is six synchronised epochs of 30 s (`ks_negotiate.c`, header):

| epoch | what happens |
|---|---|
| 0 | PROPOSE: initiator announces bin, base, trial, start epoch, participants |
| 1–4 | phases A1 B1 B2 A2: initiator applies base, trial, trial, base |
| 5 | REPORT from each participant; initiator scores and DECIDEs |

Each phase discards a 9 s settle period. It then averages P/u_ref³ in 3-s
blocks, where u_ref is the shared free-stream reference. A trial's contrast
is mean(B) − mean(A), re-referenced to the trial's mean u_ref³. The ABBA
order cancels any linear drift of the wind exactly. Only one colour class of
the conflict graph initiates per round, so no two trials share a
participant. If two nodes briefly disagree about the sector and propose
overlapping trials, the lower id keeps its trial.

## 5. Deciding under noise: the pooled sequential test

At night, one trial rarely resolves the change it is testing. At 4 m/s and
TI 0.06, the wake model values the steps as follows:

| unit | step | array gain | calibrated SE of one cooperative score |
|---|---|---|---|
| front row | 0 → 10° | about +2.1 W | 4.8 W |
| second row | 0 → 10° | about +0.7 W | 3.1 W |

A one-shot rule ("accept if score > 1 SE") is a random walk on a nearly
flat objective. Pure noise is accepted 16 % of the time. The firmware
instead pools repeated trials of the *same contrast* (base → trial, same
bin):

    z = Σ s_k / σ_k²  /  sqrt(Σ 1/σ_k²)     (inverse-variance pooled score)

    z >  accept_z (2.0)                       → ACCEPT  (move, step × 1.5)
    z < −reject_z (1.0)  or  n = pool_max (12) → REJECT  (status quo; flip
                                                direction, halve the step
                                                after two rejects)
    otherwise                                 → HOLD    (repeat the contrast
                                                at the next turn in this bin)

**Choosing the numbers.** These settings were compared in paired 36-hour
firmware-in-the-loop development runs on identical calm-night wind
(`CHANGELOG.md`, item 5):

* first steps of 10° rather than 6°: at night, the signal grows with the
  step but the noise does not;
* 30-s phases rather than 60-s: most of the noise is slow direction wander,
  and the A–B–B–A contrast rejects slow drift in proportion to (f·T)⁴, so
  shorter phases see less of it while the trial rate doubles;
* pools of 12 rather than 6.

Together they raised cooperative energy from 5 730 Wh to 5 884 Wh, against
5 638–5 711 Wh for the selfish objective on the same wind. The campaign in
`data/simulated/` uses the chosen defaults.

An aborted trial adds nothing to the pool. The ledger books the *pooled
mean* of the contrast, not the last trial. Every DECIDED log record
(version 2) carries the pooled mean, pooled z and pool size. The Python test
`test_firmware_decisions_obey_the_pooled_rule` audits every decision of a
small in-the-loop run against the rule above.

## 6. Calibrating the noise: idle rounds as free nulls

The pooled test is only as honest as its σ. The block-based standard error
treats the blocks of a phase as independent. They are not: the wind
wanders on time scales longer than a phase (the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck direction
process of the SIL has τ = 10 min), and a waked rotor's power follows the
meandering wake of its neighbour. Experiment E5 runs the naive statistics (block SE, one-shot z > 1) on
the same wind as the calibrated firmware:

* the z-scores of *selfish* trials, whose true effects are tiny, have a
  standard deviation of **2.66**, not 1, and 41 % of them exceed |z| = 2
  against an expected 4.6 %;
* selfish units wander to a mean learned yaw of 11.5°, against 3.7° when
  calibrated;
* that drift is accidental cooperation, and it hides half of the
  objective's effect: cooperative vs selfish is +2.4 % with naive
  statistics and +4.8 % with calibrated ones (figure `results_calibration`
  in the README);
* the naive rule does not cost the cooperative array itself energy over
  48 h (+0.35 % against the calibrated one, 95 % CI +0.01 to +0.69, because
  it accepts faster). What it breaks is the experiment: the selfish control
  drifts, and the measured value of the objective halves.

A round in which a node neither initiates nor participates is, for that
node, a null experiment. Nothing it controls changes, yet the epochs are
the same synchronised A1 B1 B2 A2 phases. Every node therefore measures the
same contrast of its own power in idle rounds and keeps a running mean of
z² per stability class:

    κ² ← κ² + (min(z², 36) − κ²) / min(n + 10, kappa_memory)

It starts from `kappa_prior` = 2, which counts as 10 rounds. Every own and
reported SE is multiplied by κ ≥ 1 before use. The window is cancelled if
the node joins a trial, or if its own applied offset moves (a side flip or a
bin change), because that would be a real change, not noise.

* In the SIL, with 30-s phases, κ settles between 2.3 and 3.0 (median
  2.5, experiment E1).
* `firmware/test/test_negotiate.c::test_null_calibration_gives_unit_z` runs
  a plant whose power ignores yaw, so every trial is a true null. The
  calibrated decision z-scores come out with mean −0.03 and standard
  deviation 1.07.

`kappa_memory = 0` freezes κ at `kappa_prior`. Experiment E5 uses this to
reproduce the naive design for comparison.

## 7. What aborts a trial

| flag | meaning | fatal? |
|---|---|---|
| FEW_BLOCKS | fewer than 3 valid blocks in A or B, or an empty phase | yes |
| SECTOR | the *initiator's* bin changed during the trial | yes |
| MODE | the supervisor left NORMAL (heat, gusts, faults) | yes |
| MISSED | a participant joined late or the trial was pre-empted | yes |
| SELF | a *participant's* own applied offset changed (side flip, bin change) | yes |
| DRIFT | A1 and A2 differ by > 50 % and > 6 block SEs | no (logged) |
| NOREF | no free-stream reference for some samples | no (those samples are skipped) |

DRIFT used to be fatal. In the SIL it discarded 18 % of downstream reports,
and the discarded reports were exactly the waked rotors whose power swings as
the upstream wake meanders, where steering matters most. Merging A1 with A2
before taking the variance already gives such a trial a large SE and little
weight in the pool.

## 8. Incentive compatibility, and what the key does not stop

Frames carry a SipHash-2-4 tag under the array key. Outsiders cannot forge
proposals or credit reports. A *keyed* node can still lie. A downstream unit
that over-reports its gain makes its upstream neighbour sacrifice more. The
measured, logged ledger is the audit trail, and the passive sniffer
(`kalonda.telemetry.wire`) can cross-check reported power against each
unit's own state beacons. Mechanism design for strategic reporting
(VCG-style payments, peer prediction) is out of scope here. It is the
natural next question for this testbed: agents with local objectives on
shared physical infrastructure, where the cooperative optimum is *measured
by anemometers* rather than judged by a model.
